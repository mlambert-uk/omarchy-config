## What I do

Provide proven frameworks, structures, and language patterns for delivering feedback effectively, conducting difficult conversations with care and clarity, and maintaining psychological safety and trust.

## When to use me

**Load this skill when:**
- Delivering performance feedback (positive or developmental)
- Preparing for difficult conversations (performance issues, conflicts, career disappointments)
- Coaching someone through a challenge
- Addressing behavioural concerns
- Recognising and celebrating achievements
- Managing conflict or misalignment
- Supporting career transitions

**All people management and coaching agents should load this skill.**

## Core Principles

1. **Psychological Safety First**: Feedback requires trust. Create environment where person feels safe to be vulnerable and learn.
2. **Clarity Over Comfort**: Be direct and specific. Vague feedback prevents growth. But deliver with care.
3. **Growth Mindset**: Assume person is capable of growth and change. Frame feedback as helping them succeed.
4. **Specificity**: Feedback tied to specific behaviours, impacts, and contexts—never vague or personal attacks.
5. **Timeliness**: Deliver feedback close to the event when memory is fresh and context matters.
6. **Bidirectional**: Invite perspective and dialogue. Feedback is conversation, not pronouncement.
7. **Follow-Up**: Commitment to support and accountability after feedback is given.

## Feedback Frameworks

### 1. SBI Framework (Situation-Behaviour-Impact)

**Best for**: Developmental feedback, performance coaching, behaviour change

**Structure**:
```
Situation: [Specific context, when, where]
Behaviour: [Observable action person took]
Impact: [Concrete effect on work, team, customer, or person]
```

**Example (Positive)**:
"In yesterday's design review, you asked three clarifying questions about the API contract before committing. That helped us catch a breaking change early and saved us at least two days of rework."

**Example (Developmental)**:
"During the standup last week, when you didn't mention being blocked on the database schema approval, the team couldn't help unblock you. That left you spinning for two days. Making blockers visible helps us support each other."

**Why it works**:
- Grounds feedback in observable facts, not interpretation
- Shows genuine impact, not opinion
- Removes personal judgment
- Person can see exactly what to do differently

---

### 2. The Coaching Sandwich (Context-Behaviour-Path Forward)

**Best for**: Performance coaching, skill development, career conversations

**Structure**:
```
Context: [Here's what I see and what I value about you]
Behaviour: [Here's what I noticed recently]
Path Forward: [Here's how I'd like to work together]
```

**Example**:
"I know you care deeply about code quality—I've seen that in your meticulous reviews. Recently, though, I've noticed your PRs are sometimes delayed because you're perfecting details beyond scope. I want to help you find the balance where quality is high but unblocking teammates is also a priority. Let's talk about how to prioritise."

**Why it works**:
- Establishes that you see the person's strengths
- Specific behaviour observation without judgment
- Collaborative path forward, not top-down directive
- Invites dialogue and partnership

---

### 3. The Radical Candour Quadrant (Caring Personally + Caring About Work)

**Best for**: Building high-trust relationships, differentiated feedback, performance management

**Framework**:
```
Axis 1: Care About Results (Scale: Low → High)
Axis 2: Care Personally (Scale: Low → High)

Quadrant 1 (Low Results, Low Personal): Ruinous Empathy
  → Feedback withheld; person doesn't improve
  → "I don't want to hurt their feelings"
  
Quadrant 2 (High Results, Low Personal): Obnoxious Aggression
  → Feedback delivered without care
  → Demoralises; damages trust and relationship
  
Quadrant 3 (Low Results, High Personal): Manipulative Insincerity
  → Feedback appears caring but avoids truth
  → Prevents growth; builds false intimacy
  
Quadrant 4 (High Results, High Personal): Radical Candour ✓
  → Direct, honest feedback delivered with genuine care
  → Shows you want them to succeed
  → Builds trust and enables growth
```

**How to Apply**:
1. **Care Personally**: Show genuine interest in their success, growth, and wellbeing. Share why you care.
2. **Care About Results**: Be direct about what needs to change or improve. Don't soften truth out of discomfort.
3. **Balance**: "I'm telling you this because I genuinely want you to succeed. Here's what I've observed..."

**Example**:
"I care about you and your future here. I've noticed you're responding defensively to feedback in code reviews, which is pushing teammates away. I think you're capable of better. Let's work together to change this pattern, because it's getting in the way of your growth and relationships."

### 4. DESC Framework (Describe-Express-Specify-Consequences)

**Best for**: Conflict, behaviour concerns, difficult conversations where clarity is essential

**Structure**:
```
Describe: [Objective, factual description of behaviour]
Express: [Your feelings, impact, perspective]
Specify: [Clear request for change]
Consequences: [What success looks like; impact if unchanged]
```

**Example**:
"Over the past month, I've noticed three instances where you've committed code without peer review—Monday's database migration, Wednesday's auth changes, and Friday's API modifications. I'm concerned because these are high-risk areas where peer review prevents production incidents. I need you to commit to code review for all changes in these domains. When you do this, we'll reduce risk and you'll develop faster by learning from teammates. If this pattern continues, we'll need to escalate to a formal performance plan."

**Why it works**:
- Objective facts first, not accusation
- Validates emotional reality
- Crystal clear expectation
- Explains stakes without threat

## Difficult Conversation Frameworks

### Before the Conversation

**1. Preparation Checklist**:
- [ ] **Confirm facts**: Verify what happened; avoid assumptions
- [ ] **Check your emotion**: Are you calm enough to lead? Don't have conversation in anger
- [ ] **Get context**: What might be happening on their side? Any extenuating factors?
- [ ] **Define outcome**: What do you want to achieve? (Change? Understanding? Resolution? Consequence?)
- [ ] **Choose privacy**: Schedule 1:1, private space, no interruptions
- [ ] **Plan time**: Allow time for dialogue; don't rush
- [ ] **Prepare examples**: Specific, recent examples (not patterns from months ago)
- [ ] **Assume good intent**: Approach as problem-solver, not judge

**2. Mental Framing**:
- This person is capable and will likely want to understand
- My job is to provide clarity, not punishment
- I need to listen as much as tell
- This conversation is an investment in their growth and our relationship

---

### During the Conversation

**Opening**:
```
"I want to talk about [specific situation]. I care about you and I want to understand 
what's happening. I've noticed [specific behaviour], and I want to make sure I'm 
seeing this right. Can you help me understand what's going on?"
```

**Listening Phase** (40-50% of conversation):
- Ask open questions: "Help me understand..." / "Walk me through..."
- Listen for context: stress, role clarity, capability gaps, resource constraints
- Validate emotion: "I hear that you feel frustrated"
- Avoid defensiveness: "You might be right" / "I hadn't thought of that"

**Clarity Phase**:
Use SBI or DESC to state observation clearly:
- What you observed (facts)
- Why it matters (impact)
- What needs to happen next (path forward)

**Dialogue Phase**:
- Invite questions: "What questions do you have?"
- Explore solutions together: "How could we approach this?"
- Check understanding: "How are you feeling about this?"
- Agree on specifics: "So by next Friday, you'll..."

**Closing**:
```
"I appreciate you hearing me. I believe in you and I want to see you succeed. 
Let's check in on [date] to see how this is going. I'm here if you need support."
```

---

### Handling Common Difficult Moments

**If they get defensive**:
- Pause. Don't match energy.
- Validate: "I understand this is hard to hear"
- Refocus on behaviour, not character: "I'm not questioning your intentions. I'm concerned about the impact."
- Give time: "Let's take a moment. You don't have to respond now."

**If they disagree with your observation**:
- Ask for their perspective: "Tell me how you see it"
- Look for grain of truth in their view
- You don't have to convince them immediately: "We see this differently. Here's what I need to see change."
- If fact-based: "Let me check the logs/data and we can circle back"

**If they get emotional (tears, anger)**:
- Normalise: "It's okay. Take a moment."
- Sit with silence. Don't rush to fix it.
- Offer tissue/water if appropriate
- Reassure: "This is hard. That doesn't change my commitment to you"
- Don't abandon the conversation, but give space

**If they shut down**:
- Give them permission to think: "This is a lot. You don't have to have answers now"
- Clarify what you need: "Here's what matters to me: [specific outcome]"
- Schedule follow-up: "Let's talk again on [date]. Think about what you need from me"
- Don't let it drop: Follow up is your job

## Recognition & Celebration Practices

### Why Recognition Matters

Recognition:
- Reinforces desired behaviours
- Builds psychological safety and trust
- Creates positive feedback loops
- Costs nothing but attention
- More powerful than once-yearly reviews

### Recognition Framework

**1. Timely Recognition** (within days, ideally same day):
```
"I noticed that [specific action] and wanted to thank you immediately. 
That [specific positive impact]. It means a lot."
```

**2. Specific Recognition** (not vague praise):
❌ "Great work on the project"  
✅ "Your architecture review caught three scalability issues before we deployed. That prevented what could have been a critical incident."

**3. Impact Recognition** (connect to what matters):
Show how their work affects:
- Team dynamics and trust
- Customer experience and outcomes
- Product quality and reliability
- Company mission and strategy
- Their own growth and learning

**4. Public vs. Private**:
- **Public recognition**: Wins, major contributions, behaviours you want to reinforce across team
- **Private recognition**: Personal milestones, vulnerabilities, growth moments
- **Both matter**: Use both

**5. Peer Recognition**:
Create space for teammates to recognise each other:
- Team standups: "Shout-outs?"
- Retros: "Who did something great this week?"
- Slack channel: #gratitude or #wins
- Regular team celebrations

### Recognition Examples

**Technical Excellence**:
"Your refactor of the payment service reduced latency by 40% and eliminated three categories of errors. That's exactly the kind of architectural thinking we need."

**Collaboration**:
"The way you mentored the junior engineer through their first PR—thorough, encouraging, patient—shows what our collaboration value looks like. Thank you."

**Problem-Solving**:
"You took complete ownership of debugging that production issue. You didn't wait for help, you didn't pass it off, you solved it. That kind of initiative matters."

**Growth**:
"I noticed you volunteered for that infrastructure project even though it was outside your usual area. You learned the system in a week. That takes courage and grit."

**Values in Action**:
"You raised a hard truth in the design review yesterday. That could have been uncomfortable, but you did it respectfully and constructively. That's integrity."

## Cultural Sensitivity & Inclusion

### Feedback Across Cultures & Backgrounds

Feedback delivery approaches vary across cultures. Be mindful:

**Direct Feedback Culture** (Northern Europe, Australia, USA):
- Explicit, specific, timely feedback expected
- Softening is sometimes seen as insincerity
- Focus on behaviour and impact
- Example: "This approach won't work. Here's why..."

**Harmony-Oriented Feedback Culture** (East Asia, Southern Europe, Latin America):
- Indirect, relational framing often preferred
- Building and maintaining relationship important
- Private feedback preferred over public
- Example: "I see value in your thinking. To build on it further, we might consider..."

**Approach**:
1. Ask: "How do you prefer to receive feedback?"
2. Adapt: Don't assume cultural preference by ethnicity; follow individual guidance
3. Respect: Acknowledge difference; don't dismiss as oversensitivity
4. Bridge: "I want to be direct because I value you. [Specific feedback]. How does that land?"

### Potential Biases in Feedback

Be aware of unconscious bias:
- **Gender bias**: Women often criticised for confidence; men for collaboration. Provide same feedback regardless.
- **Accent bias**: Don't criticise communication unless it genuinely impedes clarity.
- **Neurodiversity bias**: Different styles (thinking, pace, communication) aren't deficits.
- **Age bias**: Don't assume capability or learning ability based on age.
- **Race/ethnicity bias**: Be extra careful feedback isn't influenced by identity; stick to behaviour.

**Practice**: Review feedback you're about to give. Ask: "Would I say this to someone from [different background]?"

## Handling Defensive Reactions

### Why People Get Defensive

- Feedback threatens identity or security
- Person doesn't feel safe or trusted
- Feedback seems personal, not behavioural
- Person wasn't heard or invited to share perspective
- Pattern of feedback without recognition or support

### De-Escalation Strategies

**1. Validate Before Correcting**:
```
"I hear you. That sounds frustrating. Let me also share what I'm seeing..."
```

**2. Separate Behaviour From Character**:
```
"I'm not questioning your intent or your character. The impact I'm seeing is [specific]. 
Can you help me understand what was happening?"
```

**3. Invite Their Perspective**:
```
"I've shared my observation. I want to understand yours. What was going on from your view?"
```

**4. Acknowledge Valid Points**:
```
"You're right that [valid point]. AND I'm also concerned about [your point]. 
Let's figure out how to address both."
```

**5. Slow Down**:
```
"This is important. Let's not rush. Take time to think about this. 
We can circle back when you're ready."
```

**6. Reassure About Relationship**:
```
"I'm telling you this because I want you to succeed. This doesn't change my respect for you 
or my commitment to your growth here."
```

## Follow-Up & Accountability

### The 80% Rule

Feedback isn't complete without follow-up. The conversation is 20%; supporting change is 80%.

**Immediate Follow-Up** (1-3 days):
- Summarise conversation in writing if complex
- Provide resources or support if needed
- Check in casually: "How are you thinking about what we discussed?"

**Check-In Follow-Up** (1-2 weeks):
- Scheduled check-in: "Let's see how this is going"
- Notice and name progress: "I've seen you do X. That's exactly what I meant."
- Adjust support if needed: "What would help?"

**Progress Follow-Up** (Ongoing):
- Continue recognising progress
- If not improving: have follow-up conversation without anger
- If improving: celebrate and reinforce

### Supporting Change

Don't just tell someone to change; support them:
- **Clarify**: They understand what success looks like
- **Resources**: Provide tools, training, or mentoring
- **Role model**: Show the behaviour you're asking for
- **Peer support**: Connect them with teammate who excels in this area
- **Remove barriers**: If system/process makes change hard, fix the system

## Documentation Best Practices

### When to Document

Document when:
- Formal feedback (performance plan, formal warning)
- Repeated concern after multiple conversations
- Escalation or investigation
- Requirement for compliance/HR

### How to Document

**Keep records**:
- Date and time
- What was discussed (facts, not interpretation)
- What was agreed
- Follow-up needed

**Example**:
```
Date: 15 Jan 2026
Attendees: Manager, Employee
Topic: Code review commitment

Discussion:
Employee agreed to submit all changes >10 files for peer review before merge.
Employee shared concern about throughput impact. Agreed to revisit timing in 2 weeks.

Follow-up:
- Check-in scheduled: 29 Jan 2026
- Manager to monitor PR sizes and review turnaround
- Employee to flag any blockers
```

**Don't document**:
- Harsh language or character judgments
- Interpretation beyond facts
- Anything you wouldn't be comfortable sharing with HR

## Feedback Delivery Dos & Don'ts

### DO

✓ **Be specific**: "You missed the design review" not "You're unreliable"  
✓ **Be timely**: Within days, while context is fresh  
✓ **Be kind**: Deliver with genuine care for their growth  
✓ **Listen first**: Ask, understand, then share  
✓ **Assume good intent**: Believe they care and will improve  
✓ **Follow up**: Support change, don't just comment and leave  
✓ **Recognise growth**: Notice and name progress  
✓ **Keep it private**: Developmental feedback 1:1  
✓ **Get curious**: Ask questions, don't just pronounce judgment  
✓ **Stay calm**: If you're angry, wait  

### DON'T

✗ **Ambush**: Don't surprise someone with feedback in public or without context  
✗ **Generalise**: "You always..." or "You never..." (probably not true, triggers defensiveness)  
✗ **Mix old and new**: Bring up multiple issues from months ago (pick one)  
✗ **Make it personal**: Attack character, not behaviour  
✗ **Withhold**: Thinking you're being kind by not sharing (actually unkind)  
✗ **Assume perspective**: "You don't care" (you don't know their internal state)  
✗ **Compare**: "Why can't you be like [colleague]" (demoralising, creates resentment)  
✗ **Deliver and disappear**: Give feedback then vanish; that's abandonment  
✗ **Wait for crisis**: Address early, don't stockpile and explode  
✗ **Document everything**: Only document formal matters; keep most feedback conversational

## Summary Checklist

Before delivering any feedback:

- [ ] **Fact-check**: Do I have accurate information?
- [ ] **Assume good intent**: Do I believe they care and can grow?
- [ ] **Choose framework**: SBI? Coaching Sandwich? Radical Candour? DESC?
- [ ] **Prepare specifics**: Can I give a concrete example?
- [ ] **Check emotion**: Am I calm and caring?
- [ ] **Plan privacy**: Is this a safe, private conversation?
- [ ] **Plan time**: Have I allocated enough time?
- [ ] **Define outcome**: What do I want to happen?
- [ ] **Prepare to listen**: Will I ask questions and really hear?
- [ ] **Commit to follow-up**: Will I support change over time?

**Remember**: Feedback delivered with genuine care and specificity is one of the most powerful gifts you can give someone's career.
