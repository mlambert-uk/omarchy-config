## Agenda Prep — What to Surface

When preparing a 1:1 agenda, pull the following from previous notes and personal records:

- **Outstanding action items** — owner, status, any that are overdue
- **Topics carried forward** from previous meetings
- **Current work context** — project status, blockers, recent deliverables
- **Personal context** — anything relevant from the personal record
- **Manager actions** — any commitments made by the manager that need following up
- **Theme for this meeting** — if a weekly/monthly theme is set, note it

Present these as agenda topics only. Do not add guidance on how to handle them.

---

## Agenda Format

Each agenda section should contain:

- The topic
- Relevant context from previous notes (factual, one line)
- Suggested questions where useful — phrased as options, not instructions

Do not include:

- Manager notes on tone, approach, or technique
- Coaching guidance or frameworks
- Assessments of the report's behaviour or patterns
- Recommendations on how to handle the conversation

This skill provides expertise in conducting effective 1:1 meetings, from preparation and structure to note-taking, action tracking, and building strong manager-report relationships.

## 1:1 Meeting Fundamentals

### Purpose of 1:1s

**Primary Goals**:

```markdown
## What 1:1s Are For

✅ **Relationship Building**

- Build trust and rapport
- Understand motivations and concerns
- Create psychological safety

✅ **Career Development**

- Discuss growth and aspirations
- Identify skill gaps
- Plan development opportunities

✅ **Feedback Exchange**

- Give and receive feedback
- Course-correct early
- Celebrate wins

✅ **Problem Solving**

- Remove blockers
- Address concerns
- Navigate challenges

✅ **Alignment**

- Clarify priorities
- Ensure understanding of goals
- Discuss team/company updates

❌ **What 1:1s Are NOT For**

- Status updates (use standup/async)
- Technical deep-dives (schedule separately)
- Emergency firefighting (handle immediately)
```

### Cadence & Duration

**Recommended Schedule**:

```markdown
## 1:1 Frequency

**Direct Reports**:

- 30 minutes weekly (preferred)
- 45-60 minutes biweekly (minimum)
- Never less than biweekly

**New Hires** (first 90 days):

- 30 minutes 2x/week
- Extra touch points for onboarding

**High Performers**:

- Don't reduce frequency (they need support too)
- May shift focus to growth/strategy

**Underperformers**:

- Increase frequency (weekly minimum)
- More structured, documented

**Remote Teams**:

- Consider more frequent, shorter sessions
- Video required (not chat)
```

## Preparation Framework

### Manager Preparation

**Before Each 1:1**:

```markdown
## Manager Prep Checklist (10-15 minutes)

**1. Review Last 1:1 Notes** (5 min)

- Action items: Completed? Blocked?
- Topics discussed: Follow-up needed?
- Commitments made: Did I deliver?

**2. Observe Recent Work** (5 min)

- Recent PRs/code reviews
- Sprint velocity and quality
- Collaboration patterns
- Team feedback

**3. Identify Topics to Raise** (5 min)

- Feedback to give (positive and constructive)
- Blockers to discuss
- Company/team updates
- Career development opportunities

**4. Check Your Energy**

- Are you rushed or distracted?
- If so, reschedule (1:1s need presence)
```

### Report Preparation

**Encourage Reports to Prepare** (optional):

```markdown
## Report Prep Template (optional - share with team if useful)

1. **What's Going Well** (wins, progress)
2. **What's Challenging** (blockers, concerns)
3. **What I Need from You** (decisions, support)
4. **Career/Growth** (optional, as needed)

**Example**:

> Going Well: Shipped search feature, mentor relationship with Alex working great
>
> Challenging: Unclear requirements on Project Phoenix, feeling overwhelmed with 3 concurrent projects
>
> Need: Clarity on Phoenix scope, help prioritizing
>
> Growth: Want to learn about distributed systems design
```

## Meeting Structure & Agendas

### Standard 1:1 Agenda

**30-Minute Structure**:

```markdown
## 1:1 Agenda Template

**Report's Topics** (15-20 min)

- Their agenda drives
- Manager listens, asks questions
- Take notes

**Manager's Topics** (5-10 min)

- Feedback
- Company updates
- Blockers to address

**Action Items & Next Steps** (5 min)

- Recap commitments
- Set deadlines
- Schedule follow-ups if needed

**Rule**: If time runs out, manager's topics get cut, not report's
```

### Alternating Focus Areas

**Rotate Through These Themes**:

```markdown
## Monthly 1:1 Themes

**Week 1: Work & Performance**

- Sprint progress
- Quality and velocity
- Collaboration
- Feedback

**Week 2: Career & Growth**

- Goals progress
- Skills to develop
- Stretch opportunities
- Long-term aspirations

**Week 3: Team & Relationships**

- Team dynamics
- Peer feedback
- Mentorship
- Communication patterns

**Week 4: Wellbeing & Balance**

- Workload and stress
- Work-life balance
- Energy and motivation
- Support needed
```

## Note-Taking Best Practices

### What to Document

**Note-Taking Framework**:

```markdown
## 1:1 Notes Template

**Date**: 2024-03-15
**Attendee**: John Smith
**Duration**: 30 min

**Topics Discussed**:

1. **Project X Status**
   - Completed authentication module
   - Blocked on design review (waiting on Sarah)
   - Needs decision on database choice by next sprint

2. **Career Development**
   - Interested in leading a project
   - Wants to improve system design skills
   - Identified Staff Engineer promotion as 12-month goal

3. **Team Dynamics**
   - Enjoying mentoring Alex
   - Finds sprint planning too long (3+ hours)

**Action Items**:

- [ ] @Manager: Unblock design review with Sarah (by 3/17)
- [ ] @Manager: Find system design course recommendation (by 3/20)
- [ ] @John: Submit proposal for leading Project Y (by 3/25)
- [ ] @Both: Discuss sprint planning length in retro

**Feedback Given**:

- Praised quality of code reviews (specific, helpful)
- Encouraged speaking up more in architecture discussions

**Follow-Up Items**:

- Check in on Project Y proposal next week
- Review system design course progress in 2 weeks

**Mood/Energy**: Positive, engaged, motivated
```

### Where to Store Notes

**Options**:

```markdown
## Note Storage Best Practices

**Option 1: Shared Doc** (Recommended)

- Google Doc or Notion page per person
- Both manager and report have edit access
- Full transparency
- Easy to reference history

**Option 2: Manager-Only Notes**

- Private notes for sensitive items
- Use for performance concerns, career plans
- Risk: Lack of transparency

**Option 3: Hybrid**

- Shared doc for most topics
- Private doc for performance issues, calibration notes

**What to Include in Shared Notes**:
✅ Action items
✅ Goals and progress
✅ Career discussions
✅ Feedback given
✅ Decisions made

**What to Keep Private**:

- Performance review ratings (before shared)
- Compensation discussions (before finalized)
- Confidential company info
- Comparison to peers
```

## Action Item Tracking

### Effective Action Items

**Format**:

```markdown
## Action Item Template

**Format**: [@Owner] [Specific Action] (by [Date])

✅ **Good Examples**:

- @Manager: Schedule pairing session with Sarah on distributed systems (by 3/20)
- @John: Complete online security course and share key learnings (by 4/1)
- @Both: Review and finalize Q2 goals (in next 1:1)

❌ **Bad Examples**:

- Think about career goals (vague, no owner, no deadline)
- Fix the bug (unclear which bug, when)
- Follow up (on what?)
```

### Tracking System

**Accountability**:

```markdown
## Action Item Follow-Up

**Start of Each 1:1**:
Review outstanding actions from previous meetings

**Status Check**:
✅ Done: Celebrate, check off
🟡 In Progress: Discuss obstacles, adjust deadline if needed
❌ Blocked: Remove blocker or reassign
🗑️ No Longer Relevant: Discard, document why

**Manager Accountability**:
If you (manager) consistently don't complete actions, you lose credibility.

- Track your completion rate
- Apologize and adjust if you miss commitments
- Don't over-commit
```

## Conversation Patterns

### Active Listening

**Techniques**:

```markdown
## Listening Skills

**Paraphrase**:
Report: "I'm feeling overwhelmed with all these projects."
You: "It sounds like you have too much on your plate right now."

**Reflect Emotions**:
Report: "I just don't think anyone listens to my ideas."
You: "That must be frustrating to feel unheard."

**Ask Open Questions**:
❌ "Are you happy with your work?"
✅ "How are you feeling about your work lately?"

❌ "Did the meeting go well?"
✅ "What went well in the meeting? What was challenging?"

**Silence**:

- Pause after report speaks
- Give space to think
- Don't rush to fill silence
```

### Coaching Questions

**Powerful Questions**:

```markdown
## Coaching Question Bank

**For Problem-Solving**:

- "What have you tried so far?"
- "What would you do if you could decide?"
- "What's the smallest step you could take?"

**For Growth**:

- "What would stretch you just beyond your comfort zone?"
- "What skills do you want to develop?"
- "What does success look like for you?"

**For Conflict**:

- "Help me understand their perspective."
- "What outcome do you want?"
- "How can I support you in resolving this?"

**For Career**:

- "Where do you see yourself in 2 years?"
- "What kind of work energizes you?"
- "What would you do if you weren't afraid of failing?"
```

### Feedback Delivery

**In-the-Moment Feedback**:

```markdown
## Feedback in 1:1s

**Positive Feedback** (Be specific):
❌ "Great job on that project."
✅ "Your documentation on the API redesign was excellent. It helped
three other teams understand the changes quickly and avoid
breaking changes. That's exactly the kind of technical leadership
we need from senior engineers."

**Constructive Feedback** (SBI Model):
"In yesterday's standup (Situation), you didn't mention you were
blocked on the database migration (Behavior). This delayed the team
finding out about the issue by 24 hours (Impact). Going forward,
please flag blockers immediately in standup or Slack."

**Asking for Feedback**:
"What can I do to better support you?"
"What's one thing I should stop/start/continue doing?"
"How am I doing as your manager?"
```

## Common 1:1 Challenges

### When Reports Don't Prepare

**Problem**:

```markdown
## "I don't have anything to talk about"

**Response Options**:

**Short-term**:
"That's okay. Let me share what I've observed and we can discuss."
[Bring your prepared topics]

**Long-term**:
"I've noticed we often don't have much to discuss. For our 1:1s to be
valuable, I'd like you to come with at least 2 topics. Would that be helpful?"

**If Pattern Continues**:

- Discuss importance of 1:1s
- Ask what would make them more valuable
- Consider reducing frequency (but document why)
```

### When You're Too Busy

**Temptation to Cancel**:

```markdown
## "I'm too busy for 1:1s"

**Reality Check**:
❌ Canceling 1:1s sends message: "You're not a priority"

✅ **Better Options**:

1. Shorten meeting (15 min) but keep it
2. Reschedule within same week
3. Ask: "Is there anything urgent?" If no, reschedule

**Prevention**:

- Block 1:1 time as "untouchable" in calendar
- Schedule buffer before/after (don't rush)
- Batch other meetings away from 1:1 times
```

### Difficult Topics

**Addressing Performance Issues**:

```markdown
## Bringing Up Problems

**Don't Wait**:
❌ Saving up feedback for quarterly review
✅ Address issues in next 1:1

**Be Direct**:
"I want to talk about something I've observed that concerns me."

**Specific Examples**:
"In the last three sprints, you've missed your commitments.
Let's talk about what's happening and how we can get back on track."

**Collaborative**:
"Help me understand what's getting in the way."
"What support do you need to improve this?"
```

## Skip-Level 1:1s

### Purpose

**Why Skip-Levels Matter**:

```markdown
## Skip-Level Goals

**For Leader**:

- Understand team health
- Identify systemic issues
- Build relationships
- Spot talent

**For Report**:

- Access to senior leader
- Voice concerns
- Get broader context
- Career visibility
```

### Structure

**Skip-Level Format**:

```markdown
## Skip-Level 1:1 Agenda (30 min)

**Introduction** (if first meeting):
"Tell me about your role and what you're working on."

**Core Questions**:

1. "What's going well on the team?"
2. "What could be better?"
3. "Is there anything blocking you?"
4. "Do you have what you need to be successful?"
5. "How is [Your Manager] doing as a manager?"

**Career Discussion**:
"Where do you want to grow? How can I help?"

**Close**:
"Anything else you want me to know?"

**Note**: Don't make promises, but do follow up
```

**Skip-Level Best Practices**:

```markdown
## Do's and Don'ts

✅ **Do**:

- Schedule regularly (quarterly minimum)
- Ask about manager's effectiveness
- Listen more than talk
- Follow up on concerns raised
- Share themes (not individuals) with manager

❌ **Don't**:

- Undermine the direct manager
- Make commitments without checking with manager
- Share confidential info about individual
- Skip follow-up
```
